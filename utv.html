Machine vision + individual nozzle control on a John Deere UTV

Equipped a John Deere Gator UTV with a spray implement fitted with Pentair Hypro ProStop-E electric nozzle bodies for individual nozzle on/off control, driven by a machine vision camera. A color-segmentation model detects orange targets on the ground.

Built the bridge between the machine vision system and the implement: C++ firmware converts each machine vision detection into ISOBUS (CAN) messages that the ProStop-E implement controller executes on the matching nozzle.

Field tests validated the vision-to-ProStop-E communication chain and measured how travel speed affects detection-to-spray latency.

System overview

ISOBUS-compliant ECU · Raspberry Pi

A Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (quad-core, 8 GB) whose four USB ports connect up to four machine vision computers. A Waveshare 2-channel CAN HAT handles the bus: one channel goes to the tractor's ISOBUS / CAN bus through a 4-pin Deutsch connector, the second to a spare 4-pin Deutsch connector for any future CAN network. A DS3231 RTC on top of the HAT keeps time while the Pi is unpowered.

A 2-pin Deutsch power inlet feeds a 12 V → 5 V DC-DC converter for the Pi's USB-C input and a 12 V blower fan that cools the board. The unit is housed in an IP65 enclosure (6.2" × 3.5" × 2.4") with all cables leaving through a ½" cable gland.

Non-ISOBUS ECU · Arduino

Two Arduino Mega 2560 boards. One talks to the machine vision system; the other carries a Seeed CAN-BUS Shield V2.0. The TX3 pin of the vision-side Mega is linked to RX0 of the CAN-side Mega through SPL-1 lever wire connectors, so each board can be unplugged for firmware uploads. The CAN-side Mega joins the tractor's ISOBUS / CAN bus through a 4-pin Deutsch connector.

A 2-pin Deutsch connector feeds the barrel jack of the vision-side Mega, and its 5 V and ground are shared with the CAN-side Mega, so both boards run from a single power inlet. Everything sits in an IP65 enclosure (9.4" × 6.3" × 4.7") with all cables leaving through a ½" cable gland.
